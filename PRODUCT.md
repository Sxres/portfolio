# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is potential collaborators looking for someone who can turn ambitious ML and AI ideas into complete working systems. They should quickly understand both Dragos's technical range and the personality behind the work, then feel that reaching out could lead to an interesting project together.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio presents Dragos Sorescu as a creative ML/AI developer who ships full-stack systems, not isolated models or experiments. Success means a visitor is impressed by the site itself, explores the project evidence, and contacts Dragos to collaborate.

## Positioning

Dragos combines machine-learning depth with the ability to design, build, and ship the complete system around it.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: email Dragos about collaborating.
- Secondary CTA: connect with Dragos on LinkedIn.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "I build cool stuff."
- Belief ladder: Dragos has original ideas; he can build the ML core; he can turn it into a complete working system; he would be enjoyable to collaborate with.
- Proof on hand: project demos and imagery in `static/thumbnails/`, linked GitHub repositories, and `static/Dragos Sorescu Resume.pdf`. No testimonials, awards, or client logos are currently available.

## Brand Personality

Creative, clean, and out-of-the-box. The experience should feel precise and easy to navigate while still delivering a memorable moment of surprise. The restrained structure and work-led presentation of [Matthäus Jandl](https://www.matthaeusjandl.com/) and [Tamaki Yoshida](https://www.tamakiyoshida.com/) are references, especially where clean organization leaves room for a distinctive visual system and playful interaction.

## Anti-references

The portfolio must not feel like a corporate résumé, a templated SaaS landing page, a hacker-terminal costume, or generic AI-generated design. Avoid category clichés, decorative technical tropes, and motion that exists only to advertise itself.

## Design Principles

1. **Make the portfolio part of the proof.** The craft, behavior, and originality of the site should demonstrate the same ability to ship that the project copy claims.
2. **Lead with complete systems.** Show how each idea became a working end-to-end result, not merely a list of tools or model names.
3. **Create surprise inside clarity.** Keep navigation and reading effortless, then earn memorable moments through a few distinctive ideas.
4. **Show the person behind the systems.** Balance technical credibility with curiosity, approachability, and signs that Dragos would be enjoyable to build with.
5. **Make collaboration the natural next step.** Give visitors enough evidence to become interested, then make email and LinkedIn easy to find without turning the experience into a sales funnel.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus states, semantic structure, readable contrast, descriptive alternative text, and a complete reduced-motion experience. The site should remain understandable and distinctive without relying on color or animation alone.
